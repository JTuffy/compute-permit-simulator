\paragraph{Feedback-Driven Compliance: the ratchet needs ignition.}
\label{sec:sensitivity_dynamic}

Reputation escalation is the operative lever: compliance rises from 25\% (the $Q/N$ floor at $\varepsilon = 0$) to 62\% at $\varepsilon = 1$ and plateaus near 77--84\% for $\varepsilon \geq 2$, with most of the gain in the first unit of escalation (Figure~\ref{fig:dynamic_reputation_escalation}). The flat penalty raises the ceiling only gradually (Figure~\ref{fig:dynamic_penalty}), reaching 100\% at $\phi = \$300$\,M --- the point at which the expected penalty first exceeds the highest valuation in this scenario's economy ($v_i \sim \text{Uniform}[\$40\text{\,M}, \$60\text{\,M}]$). The joint surface (Figure~\ref{fig:dynamic_heatmap}) adds the design's operating requirement: at $\pi_0 = 5\%$ compliance stays near the supply floor regardless of $\varepsilon$, because audits are too infrequent to initiate the ratchet; above $\pi_0 = 25\%$, $\varepsilon \geq 1.5$ reliably produces compliance above 90\%. Escalation intensity cannot compensate for insufficient detection frequency.
